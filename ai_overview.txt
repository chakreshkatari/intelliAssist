Artificial Intelligence: An Overview

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the field of computer science focused on building systems
that can perform tasks which normally require human intelligence. These tasks include
understanding natural language, recognizing images, making decisions, and learning from
experience.

Machine Learning, a subset of AI, allows systems to learn patterns from data rather than
being explicitly programmed with rules. Deep Learning, a further subset of Machine Learning,
uses multi-layered neural networks to model complex patterns in large datasets. Deep
Learning has driven major advances in computer vision, speech recognition, and natural
language processing over the last decade.

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is the branch of AI concerned with enabling computers to
understand, interpret, and generate human language. Modern NLP systems rely heavily on
Transformer architectures, introduced in the 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need". The
Transformer architecture uses a mechanism called self-attention to weigh the importance of
different words in a sentence relative to one another, which allows models to capture
long-range dependencies in text far more effectively than earlier recurrent neural network
approaches.

BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) is a landmark Transformer
model released by Google in 2018. Unlike earlier language models that read text in one
direction, BERT is trained bidirectionally, allowing it to build a deeper understanding of
context. BERT and its many derivatives, including sentence-transformer models, are widely
used today to convert text into dense numerical vectors called embeddings. These embeddings
place semantically similar pieces of text close together in vector space, which enables
semantic search: finding documents that are conceptually related to a query even when they
do not share the same exact keywords.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is an architecture that combines a retrieval system
with a generative language model. Instead of relying purely on knowledge memorized during
training, a RAG system first retrieves relevant chunks of text from an external knowledge
base, typically stored in a vector database such as FAISS or ChromaDB, and then supplies
those chunks as context to a generative model when producing an answer. This approach
reduces hallucination, allows the system to cite its sources, and lets the knowledge base be
updated without retraining the underlying model.

A common alternative to embedding-based semantic search is TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse
Document Frequency), a classical statistical technique from information retrieval. TF-IDF
represents documents as sparse vectors of weighted word frequencies and measures similarity
using cosine similarity. TF-IDF is fast, requires no training, and works well for exact
keyword matches, but it cannot capture semantic meaning or synonyms the way BERT-based
embeddings can.

Sentiment analysis is another common NLP task, in which a system classifies the emotional
tone of a piece of text as positive, negative, or neutral. Intent detection is a related task
that identifies what a user is trying to accomplish with a piece of text, such as asking a
question, requesting a summary, or making a complaint.
